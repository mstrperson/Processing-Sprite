Lesson 2

Draw Your Character

Build the character that will star in everything you make from here on.

TimingOne period (~50 min). Can stretch to two if students are deep in it.
New ideaWriting your own class with extends Blob
Builds onLesson 1 — setup(), draw(), and making a Blob.
StandardCreate a custom extends Blob class and override drawSprite() to draw a character anchored at pos.x/pos.y.

Warm-up (~5 min)

From last time: what are the two magic functions? And what five pieces of information does a Blob need to be created?

Direct instruction (~10 min)

Making a new tab

Do this on the projector. The arrow at the top-right of the tab bar → New Tab. Name it MyCharacter with no extension.

Students miss this arrow constantly. It is small and it is at the far right edge. Point at it twice.

Frame extends in exactly one sentence

"Your character is built on top of Blob. It already knows how to move, wrap around the screen, and detect collisions. All you add is what it looks like."

Do not use the word *inheritance*. Do not explain abstract. Do not go deeper than that sentence, even if a student pushes.

The super() line

"super is the handshake line. Copy it exactly, and just fill in a radius that feels right for your character's size. The color there is a backup — you are going to draw over it anyway."

If a student asks what super actually means, that sentence is the whole answer. Say it and move on.

pos.x and pos.y — the belly button

"Think of pos.x, pos.y as your character's belly button. Every shape you draw is measured from there."

This is the single most important idea in the lesson. A character drawn at hard-coded coordinates looks identical today and breaks completely in Lesson 3. Catch it now.

Demonstrate the payoff

Build a simple face live: body circle at pos.x, pos.y, then eyes offset from there. Then manually change the position where the character is constructed and re-run — the whole face moves together.

That moment is what sells the belly-button rule. Worth the 60 seconds.

Guided activity (~20 min)

  1. Everyone creates the MyCharacter tab together. Wait for every student before moving on — this is the step that strands people.
  2. Paste in the class shell and the main sketch. Run it. Everyone should see the blue face.
  3. As a class, change one thing: the body fill. Everyone runs again.
  4. As a class, move one eye by changing its offset. Name out loud what minus and plus do on each axis.
  5. Now branch off. Students personalize; you circulate. Expect this to take the rest of the period and then some.
  6. Five minutes before the end, have students who changed the size update the radius in super(...).

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Drawing at hard-coded coordinates instead of pos.x/pos.y. Looks fine today, breaks in Lesson 3. This is the one to hunt for while circulating.
  • Modifying the super(...) line beyond the radius, or deleting it. The class will not compile.
  • Naming the tab MyCharacter.pde — Processing adds the extension, so they end up with MyCharacter.pde.pde.
  • Drawing the body last, painting over the face. Good teachable moment about draw order (which returns in Lesson 8).
  • Forgetting noFill() before arc(), which fills the smile into a solid blob.
  • A radius in super() wildly different from the visible art. It does not matter today; it matters a lot in Lesson 7.

Wrap-up (~5 min)

Ask: Show a neighbor your character and describe one shape you drew and how you positioned it.

Looking for: Students should be able to name a shape and say roughly where it sits relative to the center — "the hat is 25 above pos.y." You are listening for the offset idea, not precision.

Teaching notes

  • This lesson is the make-it-yours. Budget most of the period for personalization, not instruction.
  • Students will want elaborate characters. Encourage it, but tell them they can keep adding detail in every future lesson — today's goal is just to get *something* on screen.
  • The radius in super() becomes the collision hitbox in Lesson 7. Remind them to keep it roughly the size of their character's body.
  • Fast finishers: a second character class in another tab. It reinforces the pattern without adding a new concept.
  • This character carries the whole rest of the unit. A student who is proud of it now will be more invested in Lesson 9.

Rubric reference (no points)

Standard
Create a custom extends Blob class and override drawSprite() to draw a character anchored at pos.x/pos.y.
Getting StartedMy new tab will not compile, or nothing draws. Next step: check that the class line reads class MyCharacter extends Blob, and that the super(...) line is copied exactly — that is the handshake that connects your character to the library.
Got It WorkingA character of my own appears on screen, built from a few shapes anchored to pos.x/pos.y.
Made It Mine ⭐…and my character is clearly mine — I added or changed shapes, colors, and details (hair, hat, eyes, ears…) so it is recognizably my creation, not the starter blob.
Went Beyond…and I pushed the art further — layered or overlapping shapes, a non-circular body — or I made a second character class (a sidekick) in another tab.

⭐ Made It Mine is the goal for everyone. “Went Beyond” has no fixed list — the examples are only starting points. Going somewhere the lesson did not ask for is the whole idea.
