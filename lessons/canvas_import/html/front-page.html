Make Your Own Game

Programming with Processing and the SpriteGame library

9 lessons + 3 bonus lessons  ·  No prior coding experience needed  ·  No points, no grades

What this is

In this course you build a video game. Not a worksheet about video games — an actual game, with a character you draw yourself, that you can hand to somebody else and watch them play.

You start on the first day by putting something on the screen. In Lesson 2 you draw your character. After that, every single lesson gives that character something new: it moves, then you control it, then it gets a world to live in, then rules, then enemies. By Lesson 9 the game is yours — your character, your world, your rules.

You do not need any programming experience. Nothing here assumes you have ever seen code before.

What you will be able to do by the end

  • Write and run a Processing sketch from nothing
  • Create your own objects and tell them how to look and behave
  • Use variables, lists, loops, and if statements — because your game needed them, not because a test asked
  • Detect when things collide, and make the game react
  • Design a world with layers, terrain, and a look that is yours
  • Build a complete, playable game with win and lose conditions that somebody else can pick up and play

How this course works

Every lesson has two parts

A Lesson Page — read it, follow the steps, get the code running. It has the new words, an explanation of how the new idea works, the starter code, and things to try.

An Assignment — where you turn your work in, check yourself against the stages, and write a sentence or two about what you did.

Every lesson ends with Make It Yours

This is the part that matters most, and it is not extra credit. The starter code is a starting point, not an answer. Changing it is the assignment.

Stuck? There is a helper

Every lesson page and every assignment has a button that opens an AI helper for that lesson. It asks you questions and nudges you in the right direction — it will not just hand you the answer, because then you would not have learned anything.

Also: ask the person next to you, and ask your teacher. Both of those are allowed and both of them work.

How your work is looked at

There are no points in this course, and no grades on your work. Instead, every assignment has the same four stages. They are a map of where you are and what to try next — not a score, and not a ranking.

Getting StartedIt is not running yet. Every lesson tells you the single most likely reason and exactly what to try next.
Got It WorkingThe thing the lesson was about actually happens on your screen.
Made It Mine ⭐…and you changed it on purpose, so it is yours. This is the goal for everyone, every lesson.
Went Beyond…and you took it somewhere the lesson never asked for. There is no fixed list for this one — that is the whole point of it.

You can always go back, revise, and run it again. Nothing here is a one-shot test.
